The architecture

Your app stays on GitHub Pages; one new Supabase function does the rest

  1. The app sends a job

    The photo goes to Supabase Storage, which the app already uses.

    The app calls one function, studio-generate, with a recipe name like "carousel", never a model name.

  2. The function checks and charges credits

    It checks the Supabase login, then takes credits off in one database step.

    The fal and Google keys live only in Supabase secrets, never in index.html.

  3. It queues the generation

    Analysis goes to Gemini; pictures and video go to fal's queue with a webhook.

    The server picks the model, so a phone can't ask for an expensive one.

  4. A webhook saves the result

    It copies the file into your own Storage, since vendor links expire.

    A failed job refunds the user's credits.

  5. The app shows and shares it

    The app draws the carousel slides from the analysis and saves them as images.

    Posting goes through the phone's share sheet: no TikTok or Instagram setup needed.

Why not MCP or direct vendor APIsMCP is for AI assistants, not for buttons in your app.
  • Higgsfield's MCP lets ChatGPT or Claude make media, but each user would need their own Higgsfield account.
  • Direct vendor APIs mean one contract, key and format per vendor; fal gives Kling, Hailuo, Veo-class and image models behind one.
  • Going direct to Google for analysis and the hero image is worth it: one more key, and the image costs less than half of fal's.
Cost controlsFive guards so one user can't run up the bill.
  • Credits per user in a Supabase table, taken off before a job and refunded on failure.
  • A daily cap per user and a monthly budget switch that turns generation off for everyone.
  • Same photo and same recipe reuse the saved result instead of paying again.
Free tierCarousels are cheap enough to give away; video isn't.
  • Free: 3 carousels a month costs you roughly 12 to 27 cents a user.
  • Video and your-face video sit behind paid credits, with one free trial clip.
  • A credit could be one carousel, a clip 5 credits, a face video 15.

Phase 1: the carouselShip this first; it replaces the trip out to the custom GPT.
  • Gemini reads the photo and returns the meal, macros, a swap and recipe steps as structured data.
  • One hero image of the healthier dish from Nano Banana 2.1, $0.034, or fal's Nano Banana 2, $0.08.
  • The button appears once a Scan or Log meal result lands, so it doesn't touch the code being changed now.
Phase 2: the cooking clipAnimate the hero image into 1 to 3 short shots.
  • Start with Hailuo 2.3 Fast at $0.19 for 6 seconds, or Veo 3.1 Lite at 5 cents a second.
  • Veo can take 11 seconds to 6 minutes, so the app needs a "cooking your video" wait state and a ping when done.
  • Captions and steps are laid over by the app, like the carousel, so the text is always right.
Phase 3: video with your faceOnly after a consent screen and a deletion rule are in place.
  • Simplest: a HeyGen photo avatar intro, then the phase 2 clips; or Veo with your photo as a reference.
  • Illinois treats face-geometry scans as biometric: written notice of purpose and how long, a signed release, and a public deletion policy. Plain photos are excluded, but a face model may count. ilga.gov
  • Since Aug 2, 2026 the EU requires labelling AI-made video of real people; Veo already adds a SynthID watermark. europa.eu

Names for the section

It holds Deep analyze, your assistant, swaps and content

  1. Fuel Lab (my pick)It grows out of the Fuel section you already have, and "lab" covers both analysing and making things.
  2. Spike StudioTies straight to the LOWiSPIKE name, and "studio" says content creation the way Higgsfield does.
  3. RemixOne verb for what happens: your meal comes back as a healthier swap, a carousel or a video.
  4. Misty's KitchenPuts the companion in charge, so the section feels like part of the game, not a tool bolted on.
  5. The PassThe kitchen counter where finished plates go out; fits a place where meals leave as shareable content.
